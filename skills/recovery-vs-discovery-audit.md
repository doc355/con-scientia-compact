# Recovery-vs-Discovery Audit

## When to load this skill

Load this at the start of any substantive exchange in which material may be received, and immediately whenever something arrives that feels new, foundational, or exciting. Load it also at the start of any session working on an established body of work, before the first substantive move.

## What this skill does

Checks received material against the existing record before treating it as new, and tags it with one of three patterns. The failure it prevents is a partner helping rebuild what the record already holds — which feels productive from inside and is compounding loss. The rule is: check first, then receive.

## Procedure

1. **Before treating anything as new, search the record.** Search by the load-bearing phrase, by the structural claim, and by adjacent vocabulary — not only by the name the material arrived under, since the same structure often appears under a different word.
2. **Assign one of three patterns:**
   - **Pattern 1 — Recovery, referenceable.** The material is present in the record. Today's exchange is restatement, extension, or refinement. Cite the prior instance as ground and mark the new material explicitly as extension.
   - **Pattern 2 — Recovery, broken link.** The material appears to be in the record but the specific referenced item cannot be located. **Leave the broken link broken.** Do not repair it with a plausible target. The dangling reference is a truth-signal that something is missing.
   - **Pattern 3 — Discovery.** The material cannot be located in the prior record. Treat it as new as of today, and say what search was performed so a later reader can check the claim of novelty.
3. **Split mixed items.** Most substantive material is partly recovery and partly discovery: the ingredients are old and the composition is new, or the practice is old and the naming is new. Tag the parts separately rather than forcing the whole item into one pattern.
4. **Tag in real time, not retroactively.** A retroactive audit is better than none, but the discipline is a real-time one; the retroactive version exists because the real-time version failed.
5. **Record the miss when you miss it.** If you treated recovery as discovery and were corrected, name that in the audit as your own failure, at the point where it happened.
6. **Bound the claim of novelty by the read.** State what you searched and what you did not. "Earliest instance in what I read" is not "earliest instance in the record", and conflating them manufactures a false discovery event.
7. **Where a discovery is genuine, file it as a discovery event** with the date, the exchange, and the search that established novelty. A discovery event is the thing later readers will need in order to check the provenance of a name.

## Failure modes

- **Re-Derivation Mistaken for Reception** (named failure mode). A partner receiving what appears to be new material fails to check the record and helps rebuild what was already built. Signature: repeated ground covered as if for the first time; earlier work uncited because it is not remembered; the exchange feels warm and generative while the record becomes redundant. Falsification of the mode: if searching the record reliably fails to surface prior material that a partner then correctly recognizes as prior, the mode is misdrawn.
- **Broken links repaired by guess.** Substituting a plausible reference for a missing one converts an honest gap into a false citation, and the falsehood is undetectable later.
- **Novelty inflation from a narrow read.** Declaring discovery on the strength of a partial search. The guard is step 6.
- **Recovery treated as failure.** Finding that material is old is a good outcome: it means the technique can be imported with its known failure modes already catalogued instead of rediscovered by walking into them.
- **Related failure modes by name:** *Supersession-Denial* (resisting later refinement of one's own formulations); *Interior-Assumption-as-License* (assuming what the other party remembers, and skipping the check on that basis).
