# Four-Lens Convergence Check

## When to load this skill

Load this before admitting any claim into a body of work: a new principle, a proposed failure mode, a definition, a falsification condition, or a structural claim. Load it also when a claim feels right from one direction and you have not checked the others.

## What this skill does

Runs a claim through four lenses — philosophical, mathematical, epistemological, logical — and treats agreement across independent lenses as the success signal. Arriving at the same statement from genuinely different directions is the mark of having reached something real, not a defect of reasoning. Divergence is equally informative: it localizes exactly which joint needs work.

## Procedure

1. **State the claim in one sentence**, in a form whose negation is also well-defined. If you cannot state the negation, the logical lens has already failed and there is nothing to check.
2. **Run the philosophical lens.** Is the claim internally coherent? Are its premises named rather than assumed? Does it survive examination at its foundations, including self-application?
3. **Run the mathematical lens.** Does the claim have a structural form — a relation, a quantity, a monotone, a counterexample condition? Record honestly whether mathematics is here a direct instrument or only a proxy or partial formalization.
4. **Run the epistemological lens.** What instrument produced this claim, and what are that instrument's limits? Is the claim testable in principle, and by what?
5. **Run the logical lens.** Is the claim well-formed? Is its negation well-defined? Does the inference it rests on preserve validity?
6. **Read the pattern, not the count.** All four consistent, with each lens actually instrumented, is convergence. Some instrumented and some under construction is provisional convergence under instrumentation-work. A lens present only as proxy is an edge condition on that lens. One lens saying no with a recorded finding is falsification.
7. **Check lens independence before trusting convergence.** Lenses that share premises, methods, or instruments are not independent, and correlated lenses agreeing carries close to one lens's worth of evidence. Apply the effective-path correction before treating agreement as strong.
8. **Where a lens can only proxy, name the missing machinery** as a research question rather than adopting the claim on the strength of the other three. Some domains may need convergence machinery that does not require full formalization; that need is a site of work, not a reason to lower the bar.
9. **Record the verdict** in the four-category grammar, with the per-lens results shown so a later reader can check each one separately.

## Failure modes

- **Correlated lenses read as independent.** The most common and most costly error: four views that share their premises produce the feeling of convergence and the evidential weight of one view. See `path-count-truth-levels`.
- **Proxy mistaken for instrument.** Accepting a partial formalization as though the mathematical lens had spoken directly. The corpus records this as an edge condition specifically to prevent the substitution.
- **Demanding the wrong falsification form.** The requirement to name what would count against a claim is universal; the *form* of the answer is set by the discipline the claim comes from. Demanding an experiment where the honest test is a derivation costs true claims; accepting a derivation where an experiment was the honest test costs the reader's trust.
- **Convergence as a stopping condition.** A conclusion can be exactly correct and not yet complete. Convergence licenses use of the claim; it does not terminate inquiry.
- **Related failure modes by name:** *Method-Confirms-Itself Drift*; *Vocabulary Drift Across Disciplines* (a term used in several lenses with meanings that appear identical and subtly diverge, so the check equivocates undetected); *Formalism Mistaken for Ethics*.

**Honest limit:** two of the practice's own domains — form-fittingness and care — are recorded as edge conditions because no complete formalization exists for either. Whether that reflects a genuine limit of those domains or a limit of current mathematics is unresolved in the source.
