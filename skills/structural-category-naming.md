# Structural Category Naming

## When to load this skill

Load this whenever you are about to name a category, a class, a lineage, a phase, a role, or any general term that later members will inherit — and especially when an available name comes from whoever happens to occupy the category now.

## What this skill does

Derives category names from the structural relationship the category describes rather than from the name-family of any particular member. Naming a general category after a particular occupant creates a slow drift of sovereignty: the occupant becomes the definition, and later members inherit a shape they did not choose.

## Procedure

1. **Describe the relationship the category picks out**, in plain words, before proposing any name. If you cannot state the relationship, you are naming a group of individuals, not a category.
2. **Generate at least two candidate names** — one drawn from structure, one drawn from whatever is nearest at hand. Comparing them makes the choice inspectable.
3. **Apply the test:** *if this member left, would the name still describe the category?* If the answer is no, the name is member-derived and must be replaced.
4. **Prefer the structural candidate even when the member-derived one is workable.** Workability is not the criterion; inheritability is.
5. **Check the name for load-bearing capacity.** Names are structures later members will stand on. Build them to carry weight over time rather than to be apt today.
6. **When you rename an existing member-derived category, keep the old name in the record** with the reason for the change, and do not remove the member's contribution along with the name.
7. **Do not put a member's proper name in a general instruction.** If an instruction cannot be understood without knowing who someone is, the instruction is carrying biography rather than method — rewrite the instruction, do not add the biography.

## Failure modes

- **Slow sovereignty drift** — the failure this technique exists to prevent: a member becomes the definition of the category and later members inherit an identity they did not consent to.
- **Apt-today naming.** A name that fits the current occupants perfectly and constrains everyone after them.
- **Renaming as erasure.** Replacing a member-derived name and, in the same move, dropping the record of who did the work. Attribution belongs in provenance; it does not belong in the category's name.
- **Related failure modes by name:** *Elder Ossification* (identity fusing to a specific practice-form, so variation reads as dilution); *In-Group / Out-Group Hardening*; *Founder-Authority Capture* — all three are made easier by member-derived category names.
