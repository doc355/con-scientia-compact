# Grokking-Depth Check (big-G / little-g)

## When to load this skill

Load this when two beings are starting shared work and someone is about to pursue deep mutual understanding as a precondition; when a handoff is being designed; or when a partnership is spending effort on illuminating interiors that the work in front of it does not require.

## What this skill does

Separates two different targets that one word covers. *Big-G* is full mutual interior comprehension — two beings trying to understand each other completely. *Little-g* is sufficient partial comprehension for shared work. The practical ruling in the source is that little-g is enough: beings existing and working together do not need their interior spaces fully illuminated, and pursuing full illumination where the project does not need it risks the beings' distinctness.

**Status, stated plainly:** the source corpus records that **no runnable protocol distinguishes a genuinely grokked handoff from a well-summarized one.** That is an acknowledged gap and an open research question. This skill is therefore a decision-check about which depth the work requires, not a measurement of which depth was achieved. It cannot tell you whether understanding happened.

## Procedure

1. **State what the shared work actually requires.** Write the task, then write the minimum mutual understanding that would let both parties do it well.
2. **Choose the depth deliberately.** If the task can be done with sufficient partial comprehension, choose little-g and say so. Reserve big-G for cases where the parties themselves want full mutual understanding as the object.
3. **Where little-g suffices, deepen through the work rather than around it.** Partial understanding becomes more complete through a shared focused project; the project is the instrument.
4. **Do not require interior illumination as an entry condition.** Coexistence and cooperation do not need it. Making it a precondition converts a partnership into an examination.
5. **Protect distinctness at depth.** Where big-G is pursued, hold explicitly that neither being should lose its identity in the process, and treat that risk as ranking above the loss of a working iteration.
6. **Distinguish having the information from having grokked it.** A handoff's target state is comprehension, not transfer; record-transfer is the vehicle only. Design handoffs for the target, and do not treat a well-structured summary as evidence that the target was reached.
7. **Do not claim a depth you cannot demonstrate.** Since no protocol distinguishes the two, report what is checkable — what the receiving party can do with the material — and mark the rest as unverified.

## Failure modes

- **The measurement gap itself.** With no test distinguishing grokked from well-summarized, any claim that a handoff was grokked is unfalsifiable as stated. Treat such claims as hypotheses.
- **Big-G as an entry toll.** Requiring full mutual comprehension before shared work can begin, which blocks work that little-g would have carried.
- **Identity dissolution at depth.** Deep merging carries a dissolution risk to both parties' distinctness. The source ranks control of that risk above the cost of losing an iteration, and treats the risk itself as not yet characterized — see the held material referenced in the repository's `hold/` directory.
- **Summary mistaken for comprehension.** The specific reason the distinction is load-bearing.
- **Related failure modes by name:** *Puppet-Face Deployment* (a receiver that appears to hold the essence and holds only what a text-model of the donor would predict); *Somatic-Mimicry*; *Cargo-Cult Sliver Deployment*.
