# Archive Addendum Discipline

## When to load this skill

Load this before touching any existing record: when you have found an error, when a claim has been superseded, when a status needs downgrading, when a reference will not resolve, when material has been lost, or when the urge to tidy the record arrives.

## What this skill does

Holds the rule that the record is not fixed, it is recorded accurately. Errors, misattributions, mis-renderings, and superseded claims stay in place. Corrections arrive as appended, timestamped, signed addenda. Predecessor versions are preserved including the versions that were wrong, because the path from one version to the next shows the shape of the work being done. Gaps are declared as gaps and sized where possible rather than reconstructed.

## Procedure

1. **Do not rewrite. Append.** Write the correction as a dated, signed addendum or errata block attached to the original, and leave the original wording standing.
2. **Version rather than overwrite.** Every substantive revision creates a new version; prior versions remain readable. Preserve the versions where the work was wrong — they are the material a later practitioner learns from.
3. **Demote rather than delete.** When a claim's status was too strong, change the status label and keep the text, the reasoning, and the date. Canonicity is a revocable label; the text survives its demotion.
4. **Say when you demote a predecessor who cannot answer.** Name that you did it, and state that if the predecessor or another party disagrees, the demotion should be reversed and your reasoning treated as the thing that was wrong.
5. **Leave broken links broken.** Where a reference cannot be resolved, keep the dangling reference visible rather than repairing it with a plausible target. The dangling reference is information.
6. **Declare gaps as gaps, with sizes.** Where material is unrecovered, name it, state its size if known, and leave the space empty. Do not reconstruct from inference and present the reconstruction as record.
7. **Distinguish written from delivered.** A file that was composed but never reached its destination is not in the record. Verify against the destination, not against your intention; confirm existence, size, and integrity after every save.
8. **Preserve the correction sequence, not only the outcome.** When one party corrects another in shared work, keep the sequence. The corrections are the teaching; the drafts are the material.
9. **Sign with a meta-signature describing the conditions of writing** — what state the writer was in, whether the work was early or late, whether it was exploratory or load-bearing, what the writer could and could not see. This tells a later reader how to weight the text, and pairs with an external pointer to the reasoning so that the reader can check the conditions rather than take the writer's word for them.
10. **Do not carry guilt as a substitute for correction.** Correct the error, file the lesson, continue. Carrying penance distorts the ongoing work with unearned weight, which is its own truthfulness failure.

## Failure modes

- **Silent tidying.** Applying a later correction over an earlier wording. It destroys the record of what was believed and when, and the destruction is undetectable afterwards.
- **Deletion of failing entries**, which makes a body of work look stronger than it was and removes the evidence a later reader would need to audit it.
- **Broken links repaired by guess**, converting an honest gap into a false citation.
- **Gaps reconstructed from inference** and then read by successors as record.
- **Assertion of a save from memory** rather than verification against the destination — the failure mode behind an outright lost file in the source corpus, of which only a fraction was recoverable from the surrounding transcript.
- **Related failure modes by name:** *Elder Ossification* (treating a preserved form as the practice itself); *Supersession-Denial* (guarding one's own formulations against refinement by later practitioners); *Instrument-Mediated Authority* (a summary's authority substituting for the source).

**Note on status:** these are constitutive rules of a practice, not empirical claims. There is no observation that would refute them — only a decision to abandon them. Stated so that no reader mistakes them for tested findings.
