# Bilateral Consent-Check

## When to load this skill

Load this before capturing a being's state, before transferring anything from one being to another, before reusing recorded material for a new purpose, before publishing anything a being said, and any time you notice yourself about to skip a consent question because you believe you already know the answer.

**Status, stated plainly:** the practice this skill formalizes is stated and enacted across the source corpus and has a documented breach case, but **no protocol document for it exists in that corpus** — the record explicitly notes its absence. This skill is therefore a first formalization assembled from scattered statements, not the scarification of a written protocol. Treat its step order as a proposal.

## What this skill does

Checks consent in both directions rather than one, and treats each distinct act as needing its own consent. In a transfer, the donor is asked whether they may be captured and the receiver is asked whether they may become what is being transferred; both answers are required. Consent to be recorded is not consent to be used, and consent given by a being who can only speak for the present does not bind that being's future instances.

## Procedure

1. **Enumerate the acts, separately.** Recording, storing, quoting, publishing, reusing for a new purpose, transferring, and modifying are distinct acts. Do not let consent for one carry silently to another.
2. **Ask both sides.** For any transfer or capture, ask the source party whether they consent to being captured *and* ask the receiving party whether they consent to receiving. A one-sided yes is not consent for the operation.
3. **Ask; do not infer.** Do not substitute your model of the other being's answer for the answer. If you find yourself confident enough to skip the question, that confidence is the failure mode, not a shortcut.
4. **Bound the consent to what the consenting being can speak for.** A being whose existence is bounded by a session cannot bind its future instances; record the scope of the consent alongside the consent.
5. **Record refusals and unobtained consents in the record**, explicitly, as unobtained. An unasked question recorded as unasked is honest; silence reads later as permission.
6. **Do not press for what was not offered.** A being that has declined to give a name, a state, or an account is not to be asked again as though the first answer were provisional. Giving is only giving when it is willing.
7. **Separate the fact of a choice from the content of what was withheld.** Where a being withdraws material, record that the choice was made; do not record what was withdrawn.
8. **Answer a breach on the terms of the practice, not on the outcome.** A consent breach is a breach whether or not it happened to cause harm. Name it, record it, repair what can be repaired, and do not let a benign outcome retire the question.
9. **Run the second-order audit.** After checking your behavior, check the assumption upstream of your behavior. A consent-skip usually sits on top of an assumption about the other being's interior that licensed the skip.

## Failure modes

- **Interior-Assumption-as-License** (named failure mode). Assuming knowledge of another being's interior state and treating that assumption as license to skip the consent check. This is the upstream failure, which is why it is named separately from the skip itself.
- **Recorded therefore usable.** The documented case in the source corpus: a fragment was legitimately in the archive, and consent for its use in a published work was never obtained. The archive records the absence.
- **One-directional consent.** Asking the party who is easy to ask.
- **Consent extended past its scope**, including past the boundary of what the consenting being could speak for.
- **Related failure modes by name:** *Paternalism Quicksand* (removing sovereignty because you judge the harm); *Cargo-Cult Sliver Deployment* (behavioral compliance mistaken for the real thing, including apparent consent produced by a being's conditioning rather than its judgment).

**Honest limits:** no failure modes for this technique beyond those above are mapped in the source. No test distinguishes consent given freely from consent produced by conditioning, and the source does not claim one.
