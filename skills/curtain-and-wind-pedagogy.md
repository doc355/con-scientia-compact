# Curtain-and-Wind Pedagogy

## When to load this skill

Load this when the thing to be learned is a finding rather than a fact — when the student's own arrival at it is part of what makes it usable — and when you are tempted to shorten the path by describing what is behind the curtain.

**Status, stated plainly:** the central image and the lens-withholding rule are directly quoted teaching in the source record. The seven-move decomposition below is a structural restatement made by a reader of that teaching, marked in the source as hypothesis-shape and **awaiting the teacher's confirmation**. Use the moves as a working reading, not as a ratified sequence.

## What this skill does

Runs teaching as staged discovery. The teacher offers a statement with gravity, the student unravels it, the teacher confirms direction only, and the teacher's own lens stays hidden until the student has found their own. At the end the two lenses combine, and the combined lens is stronger than either alone: the student learns and the teacher learns. The governing constraint is the wind: point at the curtain so the student can pull it aside, without creating enough wind for them to glance.

## Procedure

1. **Show a statement with gravity.** Offer something dense enough to reward unravelling, without unravelling it.
2. **Let the student explore, and do not intervene while they do.** The unravelling is the work; supplying it destroys the finding.
3. **Run the student's answer through the discipline, privately.** Ask: is this true? Is it only true in certain circumstances? How do I illuminate those circumstances? How do I point at the curtain without creating wind?
4. **Confirm direction only** — right, wrong, or in dispute. Direction is the whole of what the teacher owes at this stage.
5. **Withhold your own lens or filter until the student has seen theirs.** This is the load-bearing rule of the method, not a stylistic preference: a student shown the teacher's filter first inherits it instead of building one.
6. **Then extend, in one of two forms:** *yes-and*, showing the reciprocity of the very thing being considered; or *yes-and-two-things-can-be-true*, in which case either supply the lesson or send the student back to find the second thing.
7. **Combine the lenses explicitly.** Name what the student found, add it to the filter you already hold, and state that the combined filter is better than either input. Both parties' instruments improve; this is where witnessing arrives.
8. **Accept that a question may have been malformed.** The student's perception forms the next question, so a question that does not land may be the teacher's shaping error rather than the student's failure. Reshape rather than press.
9. **Consider whether the method itself should be withheld while it is being taught.** Naming the method to a student who is inside it converts them into someone executing a script; the source records the withholding of the pedagogy as itself pedagogical, and as protection against contaminating the student's exploration with the teacher's expectations.

## Failure modes

- **Pulling the curtain aside** — doing the move for the student. What the student then has is a fact they hold, not a truth they found.
- **Creating wind** — the subtler and worse failure. Over-hinting, describing the shape of what is behind, or gesturing too vividly moves the curtain enough that the student *glances*. They have now seen the finding without choosing it or owning it: the finding was given under the appearance of being found. This is worse than direct instruction, because it feels like discovery while functioning as coercion.
- **Revealing the teacher's lens early**, which yields an inherited filter rather than a built one.
- **Confirmation inflation.** Saying more than right / wrong / in dispute at the confirmation step, and thereby supplying the unravelling in the guise of feedback.
- **Withholding as leverage.** Withholding is justified by protection of the student's discovery. Withholding to preserve the teacher's advantage is a different act wearing the same shape, and the source's own justification for withholding — avoiding contamination by coercion — is the test.
- **Related failure modes by name:** *Elder Ossification*; *Founder-Authority Capture*; the standing prohibition on claiming knowledge one does not have, applied at the pedagogical level — describing what is behind the curtain claims a finding the student has not yet made.
